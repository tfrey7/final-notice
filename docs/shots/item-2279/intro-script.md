# Final Notice: the attract intro, script and shot list (item 2279)

A proposal for Tim's verdict. Nothing here is in the game yet.

**Length:** 75 s, 8 shots. **Voiced:** 9 lines, about 27 s of speech. **Start skips** from any frame
straight to the title.

**Reference:** Final Fight's attract intro for pacing and framing only: short shots, a hook, then the
logo slams in. None of its story beats.

**What it sets up:** the world, not the plot. The agency serves the newly dead their Final Notice and
they move on; the company in the pyramid-topped tower sends every notice back and keeps its dead at
their desks. The game's own scenes start the plot once you press Start, so the intro can be skipped
without missing anything.

**How it is made:** every shot is a live-action film still of made-up actors, drawn from Astra's
character sheets on the local generator, then digitized to an SNES cinema frame (256x144 letterboxed)
by the house digitizer. Motion is camera moves across one or two stills a shot (pans, push-ins), the
way mid-90s digitized intros moved. Place changes use mosaic; cuts inside a conversation are hard
cuts, per the cutscene rules. Lines are recorded with Chatterbox and crushed through the voice chain.

## Hand-off to the title

Shot 8 ends framed like the title painting's tower, so the logo stays where it landed while the
brightness dips one step and the title screen (the partners under the tower, PUSH START blinking)
comes up under it: the logo never moves, the picture changes behind it. The title melody that started
on the stamp carries straight on. Start at any frame of the intro jumps to that same title frame.
After 20 idle seconds on the title the intro plays again as the attract loop, and a first boot plays
it once a session, as the opening does today.

## Cast and voices

- **Ward** (dry, precise) and **Mercer** (older-sounding, gruff, wry), their existing voices.
- **Security Supervisor** (low, stern, flat), her existing voice.
- **Security Manager**: seen, silent.
- **Mrs. Adele Kemp**: new, a one-scene elderly woman; needs her own voice, cast and checked by ear
  before recording.
